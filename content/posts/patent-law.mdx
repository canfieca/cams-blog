Upon completion of my Computer Science undergraduate degree I was contemplating a lot about what I wanted to do for the rest of my life. I was being stressed out by a lot of outside influence which was really making it hard to make up my mind. For the longest time it was difficult to shake the feeling of disappointing all of my family because they had such high hopes for me. All that being said, I am not nothing more than a man with a plan!

## Original Aspirations

As previously stated, I did my undergraduate degree in computer science so with that I thought I could become a software engineer. There are a lot of aspects about software engineering that I do like such as creative problem solving.

From my research, the evidence would suggest that as a career software engineering is a little less creative than doing personal projects. So I'm not sure if I was able to see myself doing that for the long run.

My mother wanted me to be a software engineer because she assumed that's what I wanted to do and due to a downed economy I gave up on it. Is there some sembalence of truth to that sentiment? Maybe just a little.

I could become a software engineer if I really wanted to but it may have taken some time. Parental pressure of getting something done now, now, now was causing a lot of unnecessary stress.

## The Great Pivot

During my undergraduate degree I would express my love and admiration for trial law and how I thought intellectual debate was stimulating. I would talk with my old roommate Connor about it all the time because we loved to debate. So much so that he'd regularly tell me that I was wasting my time with computer science and should pursue law.

As I am always the man with a plan I told him I can still go to law school with a computer science degree. Which is why I'm not so down about myself for thinking this is me giving up on my dreams of being a software engineer because it was never really my dream.

I never was one to live my life which too much intention, I more of a go with the flow kind of a guy. It felt more like me checking off a bucket list instead of doing the things that I wanted to do. Now I'm intending to be a patent litigator.

## Rationale

Something I struggled with was thinking well I am kind of smart so I can do what I want if I put my mind to it, so what do I want to put my mind to? As we all know the difference between justification and rationalization is when the intention comes in.

I <em><strong>justified</strong></em> my choice to undergrad as a computer scientist, meaning the intention came later. In other words, I was doing computer science and then thought to myself well I'm good with computers.

Now I'm <em><strong>rationalizing</strong></em> my decision to become a patent litigator because of my late uncle Jimmy! He too was a patent attorney and he did it for my late grandmother. Now unfortunately my late uncle passed away an unmarried man and I hated how everyone framed his situation.

He lived in California so I didn't get to visit him as much as I should've. He passed away some time ago now so I only really have faint memories of him. From the memories I do have of him I always got the impression that he was the nicest man, and to me I don't think that's such a bad way to be remembered by.

So this one's for you, the nicest man, my uncle James!
