It's no secret that the USA has had issues with mass illegal immigration. The issue prevades both sides of the aisle and people genuinely want to help. Like most political issues, we may find agreement on problems but disagree strongly when it comes to solutions.

People on the left side of the aisle believe we should help humans out that are escaping terrible situations in their home countries. Those on the right side of the aisle believe a country without borders isn't a country.

## Where do I fall?

I'd classify myself as center right on this issue. I sympathize with those who are escaping terrible situations in their home country, I do. However, the United States is seeing a lot of domestic problems in terms of the housing market, job market, inflation, homelessness, addiction just to name a few.

I'm not sure if the United States is necessarily in the position to be accepting the sick and hungry like we once were. At the end of the day we need to look out for ourselves first then we can look after others. There's a reason why on planes you're instructed to put your own mask on before helping the person next to you.

Below I will list some talking points about the issue and then I'll do some research to see if any of the talking points hold validity.

Talking Points:

- Most illegal immigrants are escaping terrible situations
- Most illegal immigrants are committing violent offenses / crimes
- Illegal immigration is a draw on our social welfare programs
- Illegal immigration makes up a majority of our labor pool

I'm no political expert nor do I have the desire to become one because of the abundance of information available to our finger tips. I'm just going to be speaking from a common sense perspective and cite a few statistics to back my claims.

## About Illegal Immigration

Illegal immigration has always been an issue in the country but has reached historic highs in recent years (Passel & Krogstad). In 2023 alone, illegal immigration reached 14 million crossings. Let's dive into the specifics.

### Most illegal immigrants are escaping terrible situations

This claim gets thrown around a lot and is often times lumped together with "legal immigration takes too long for those escaping persecution". Well this claim falls flat on its face from the onset because we already have systems in place for people legitmately fleeing danger. 

Two big ones are our "Refugee Resettlement from Abroad program" and "Seeking Asylum after Arriving in the U.S. or at a Port of Entry".

There's very specific criteria one has to meet in order to become a refugee. As defined by the Office of Refugee Resettlement: "A refugee is an individual granted refugee status...because of persecution or a well-founded fear of persecution for reasons of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group, or political opinion and unable or unwilling to return to their home country."

Now let's look at the definition of an asylee, once again defined by the ORR: "An asylee is a person who has fled his or her country and is unable or unwilling to return due to persecution...based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a social group."

As Americans there's certain core values we hold true such as the Freedom of Speech, Religion, Press, Assembly... so on and so forth. We believe that people shouldn't be persecuted for these inalienable human rights and should be granted refuge in America.

Finding an exact number to answer how many illegal immigrants qualify for those protections is quite impossible. I threw that question into good ol' ChatGPT and this is an excerpt of what it had to say:

> My best rough estimate based on available data <br /><br /> Given all the limitations, you might attempt a rough bound: <br /> <ul><li>Total undocumented ~ 11 million</li><li>A small subset of undocumented apply for asylum or equivalent protection.</li><li>Of that subset, roughly ~30-40% are successful in asylum decisions (for those decided).</li><li>CIS’s “fewer than 17% received asylum” figure suggests that of the specific dataset (border‐applicants claiming fear) fewer than ~1 in 6 succeed.</li></ul>If one were extremely generous and assumed that all applicants should have qualified (which is unrealistic), and if e.g. 10% of undocumented apply, and 40% succeed, that’s 4%. If one is more conservative (say only 5% apply, 30% succeed) that’s ~1.5%. The truth could be somewhere in that ball‐park or lower. <br /><br /> Thus a very rough ballpark might be: likely in the single‐digit percentage range of the total undocumented population who both fled conditions that could qualify and actually met the criteria and succeeded. But that’s not a precise estimate.

It should go without saying but always exercise caution when relying on AI to get information. This is supposed to be a fun little thing for me I'm not going to spend hours doing the research myself. That being said if we were take this value as true it would show only a small number of total illegal immigrants are escaping persecution. 

The reason I keep mentioning the word persecution is because that's the exact thing you must be fleeing from in order to qualify for those protections. Extreme poverty doesn't qualify a migrant for refugee or asylum. Which in my humble opinion, is most likely the plurality of people entering the country illegally.

### Most illegal immigrants are committing violent offenses / crimes

Now to bring up a talking point on the right is this notion that illegal immigrants are bringing terrible crime to this country. I believe in today's day and age headlines sell, and one that sells like hot cakes is illegal immigrants who commit heinous crimes. These do exist no one can deny it but let's take a look at the numbers and see what's real.

Of the 5,954 individuals who were arrested by U.S. Border Patrol through June of 2025, 469 of them were convicted of violent offenses. Which would make up about 8% illegal immigrants who get convicted of crimes. 

Another issue with illegal immigration comes illegal drug trafficking which 649 illegal immigrants were convicted of (~11%). While drug-related crime convictions doesn't necessarily mean one committed violence but anyone with two eyes and ears knows about the violent, <strong><em>violent</em></strong> nature of the drug ring and cartels. 

These numbers are reflective of Donald Trumps presidency and his strong stance in opposition for mass illegal immigration. To compare them to former President Joe Biden's numbers would show a decrease in total crimes committed by illegal immigrants. Which some would say the illegal immigrant crime numbers are so low because Trump isn't letting as many in, which is the point in having strong border protection.

As you can see just by way of having stronger border protection we can decrease crimes that are being committed by illegal immigrants. I don't think it's a stretch of the imagination to say Biden's adminstration was more lax on immigration laws than Trump's presidency. These numbers would reflect that very sentiment.

All this being said, however, obviously I don't think it's fair to generalize an entire population of people (illegal immigrants) and say most or even a great number of them are committing violent crimes. Simply by looking at the numbers for 2023, which earlier I mentioned there was 14 million illegal immigrants which only 1,254 of whom committed violent crimes. Which would make up an astronomically low .009%.

### Illegal immigration is a draw on our social welfare programs 

There are social welfare programs that exist and many illegal immigrants are taking advantage of them. This is the talking point that I most closely agree with and do think it's a significant problem. The evidence would suggest illegal immigrants draw more on our social welfare programs than they pay in taxes. Significantly more as well.

Fiscal impact is a measurement of the amount one pays in taxes compared to how much they're drawing from those taxes. Taxes go to fund things like public schools, roads, and common infrastructure. I believe it's a bad thing to let in millions of illegal immigrants who, statistically, don't contribute enough in taxes. 

Now for all of the liberals that are reading this and thinking to themselves "What about all of the billionaires that aren't paying their fair share in taxes!". You'd be absolutely correct in that assertion that the current tax policies seems to favor those who have enough disposable income to reinvest in the economy. But that's a separate issue and one that I'm not talking about right now. Maybe in the future I'll address the current tax policies in place but that's a whole other rabbit hole of information.

Back to illegal immigrants who don't pay their taxes, here's some of the key figures:

- Lifetime fiscal drain (taxes paid minus costs) for each illegal immigrant is about $68,000
- Current estimates state 59% of households headed by illegal immigrants use one or more major welfare program, compared to 39% of households headed by the U.S.-born
- Estimates of providing emergency healthcare to illegal immigrants likely total some $7 billion annually

I could go on and on but I won't and all of the sources I'm citing will be at the bottom, including the source I pulled the above numbers from. I'd really recommend reading this <a href="https://www.congress.gov/118/meeting/house/116727/witnesses/HHRG-118-JU01-Wstate-CamarotaS-20240111.pdf" target="_blank" > one specifically</a> because it's my biggest claim and highlights a lot of economic data surrounding illegal immigration.

It also explains the key reasons why this problem exists but in summary: while illegal immigrants who come here do work hard, almost all of them have jobs, they simply just don't make enough income to reach a net zero draw on our system, let alone contribute to it.

### Illegal immigration makes up a majority of our labor pool

Finally the last talking point, I didn't think this article would take as long as it did I'm currently on my third hour of writing / researching this. This sort of talking point is used in rebutal to my previous talking point. Even though I already explained that even if illegal immigrants have jobs and they are working they're still a net drain on the system.

But this talking point is categorically incorrect, a quick google search would prove it. Overall, it's estimated that 4.6% of the US workforce is illegal. Which is far from being the majority.

Another thing that gets talked about a lot surrounding this particular point is that white boys don't want to work the jobs illegal immigrants are taking. To me this has always come off as out-of-touch, smug, dismissive, and even racist in ways.

To suggest that white people don't want to work labor intensive jobs would imply we want to work, what exactly, "real" jobs. Jobs that illegal immigrants couldn't do? Jobs that illegal immigrants aren't smart enough to obtain? Jobs that illegal immigrants aren't educated enough to achieve?

Look I'm not sitting here saying you need a college degree to do labor work, you don't. But this idea that all white people are in corporate or office jobs couldn't be further from the truth. There are white people in this country that need to earn a living doing labor work, it's true, they exist. So to then demonize white people in positions where all they can do is labor work and say "Oh you shouldn't be working that job an illegal immigrant should" is a pretty crappy thing to do in my opinion.

## Moving Forward

If I were to create an elevator pitch on my views of illegal immigration it would go something like this.

> Immigrants have done great things for this country and there needs to be a way to allow all of the best immigrants into this country. Immigrants who hold our core values near and dear to their heart. However, there needs to be strict limits on how many we should let in this country, immigration laws must be enforced strictly even if that means good people will be deported, and we must stop demonizing people who believe in a strong border.

If you find any of the points I made here incorrect as always please feel free to reach out to me I welcome all discussion, discourse, and debate!
